THE $3 MILLION COFFEE SCAM

VIRAL OUTRAGE

In 1992, a 79-year-old woman named Stella Liebeck bought a 49-cent cup of coffee from a fast-food drive-thru. While sitting in a parked car, she spilled the dangerously hot coffee all over her lap, suffering third-degree burns.

She sued the restaurant. A jury ultimately awarded her nearly $3 million in damages. When the media reported the story, the public was absolutely furious. Late-night comedians mocked her mercilessly, assuming she was just a clumsy person trying to a successful company.

Most people didn't bother to of the court documents. They thought she was just trying to force the restaurant to millions for her own mistake, and they were angry the jury let her it.

But what if the corporation was actually trying to a known danger? What if they chose to to hundreds of previous injuries to maximize their profits?

AUDIO LOG: THE INVESTIGATIVE REPORT

REPORTER: The media painted Stella as a greedy scammer. But if you read between the lines of the court documents, a terrifying reality emerges.

The coffee wasn't just warm; it was kept at a staggering 190 degrees Fahrenheit (88°C) by corporate mandate, causing Stella to suffer third-degree burns requiring skin grafts. While it may be true that she spilled it, the corporation had over 700 prior complaints of severe burns. They chose to turn a blind eye to the danger.

Originally, Stella didn't ask for millions. She simply asked them to cough up $20,000 to cover her medical bills. The corporation offered her $800. Consequently, she took them to court.

The jury was so disgusted by the corporate attempt to cover up the danger that *they* assigned the $2.9 million as punitive damages—the exact amount of revenue the company made from two days of coffee sales. They refused to let them get away with it.
