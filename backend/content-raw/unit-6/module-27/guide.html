The Math Nuggets

🧮

The "S" Club (Countables)

If you can easily count it (1 apple, 2 apples), it is Countable. To make it plural, you add an **'S'** (or **'ES'** if the word ends in o, ch, sh, ss, x).

"I need three **tomatoes** and two **eggs**."

The Untouchables (Uncountables)

Liquids, powders, and big masses (water, rice, meat). You CANNOT count them. They **NEVER** take an 'S'.

"I need some **water**." (Not: two waters ❌)

How Much vs. How Many

Use **How many** for things you can count (the 'S' club). Use **How much** for the untouchables (liquids/powders).

"**How many** apples? / **How much** milk?"

The Paisa Traps

In Spanish, we say "dos panes" or "muchos dineros". In English, **Bread, Money, Advice,** and **Furniture** are UNCOUNTABLE.

"**How much** bread do we have?" (Not: how many breads ❌)

!

The Fridge List

Countables (Can add 'S')
  • ✓ Apple ➔ Apples
  • ✓ Potato ➔ Potatoes
  • ✓ Onion ➔ Onions
Uncountables (NO 'S')
  • ❌ Water / Milk / Juice
  • ❌ Rice / Sugar / Salt
  • ❌ Meat / Chicken / Bread

Paisa Secret:

Since Uncountables can never be plural, always treat them as singular for verbs! Say "**The milk IS cold**" and "**The money IS on the table**".

Measuring Nuggets

⚖️

The Container Hack

You can't count water, but you CAN count bottles! To measure uncountables, put them in a container. Use 'A ___ of'.

"I bought **three bottles of** water and **a slice of** bread."

Some vs. Any

When you don't have an exact number. Use **Some** for positive sentences (+). Use **Any** for questions (?) and negatives (-).

"I need **some** rice. Do we have **any** sugar?"

A few vs. A little

Both mean 'a small amount'. **A few** is ONLY for countables. **A little** is ONLY for uncountables.

"I bought **a few** tomatoes and **a little** salt."

A lot of

The ultimate universal lifesaver! It means 'a large amount' and it works perfectly for BOTH countables and uncountables.

"I have **a lot of** apples and **a lot of** money!"

!

The Containers

Liquids & Pastes
  • A bottle of water
  • A carton of milk
  • A jar of mayo (Frasco)
Solids & Powders
  • A slice of bread / pizza
  • A loaf of bread (La hogaza entera)
  • A bag of rice / chips
  • A bunch of bananas (Racimo)

Street Tip:

If someone offers you food and you only want a tiny bit, say: **"Just a little, please."** If they offer you cookies (countable), say: **"Just a few, please."**

The Supermarket Mission

Record a voice note checking your kitchen! Say: "We have **some** [Countable+S], but we need **a bottle of** [Liquid]. We don't have **any** [Uncountable]!"
