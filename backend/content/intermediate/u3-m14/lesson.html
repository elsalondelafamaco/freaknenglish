THE GENTLE SHIFT

READING & CHECK

"Parenting has undergone a radical transformation in the last few decades. In the past, discipline often meant fear and strict obedience; kids knew the rules and followed them unconditionally.

On the other hand, modern 'gentle parenting' focuses heavily on validating a child's emotions and explaining the reasoning behind every single rule. While this approach builds empathy, critics argue it has gone way too far.

We are increasingly seeing children who completely ignore boundaries because they have never faced a real consequence. If we negotiate every single boundary like a business deal, are we raising emotionally intelligent humans, or just entitled tyrants?"

PSYCHOLOGY 301 LECTURE

PROFESSOR: "Welcome back, everyone. Today we are examining the psychological spectrum of discipline.

On one extreme, authoritarian parenting relies heavily on strict rules and punishment. This 'do as I say' mentality creates obedience; however, it often breeds resentment and anxiety as the child gets older.

Permissive parents, on the other hand, act more like friends than authority figures. They rarely tell their kids off, fearing they might damage their self-esteem. The psychological effect of this is that, because they lack boundaries, these children often act out to subconsciously test limits.

We must find a balance; otherwise, we risk raising brats who crumble at the first sign of real-world adversity."
