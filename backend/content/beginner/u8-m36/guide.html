Experience Nuggets

✨

Have you ever...?

The ultimate interrogation question. It means "In your entire life, from birth until today, did you do this?"

"**Have you ever** eaten sushi?"

I have / I haven't

To claim an experience, use **I have**. To say you are innocent, use **I haven't**.

"**I have** seen that movie. **I haven't** read the book."

The V3 Verbs

When using 'Have', you MUST use the 3rd column of the verb list (Past Participle). No 'went', no 'ate'!

"I have **eaten**." (Not: I have ate ❌)

I have never...

The dramatic way to say "0% experience in my life". Don't use 'haven't' with 'never'!

"I **have never** broken a bone."

!

The V3 List

Irregular Traps
  • See ➔ Saw ➔ SEEN
  • Eat ➔ Ate ➔ EATEN
  • Go ➔ Went ➔ BEEN / GONE
  • Do ➔ Did ➔ DONE
Regular Verbs
  • Regular verbs are lazy. V2 and V3 are the same!
  • Play ➔ Played ➔ PLAYED

Paisa Secret:

If someone asks "Have you ever...?", answer with "**Yes, I have.**" Do NOT say "Yes, I did." Match the first word of their question!

Timeline Nuggets

⏳

The Shift (To Simple Past)

You use 'I have' to say the experience is real. But if you want to give DETAILS (when, where, why), you MUST switch to Simple Past!

"I **have been** to Paris. I **went** there in 2019 and it **was** great!"

The Exact Time Trap

Never use 'I have' with finished time words (yesterday, last year, in 2020). That is illegal!

"I **saw** him yesterday." (Not: I have seen him yesterday ❌)

Been vs. Gone

**Been** = You went and you came back. **Gone** = You went and you are STILL there.

"I have **been** to Spain (I am home now)."
"Titó has **gone** to Spain (He is in Madrid right now)."

Already vs. Yet

**Already** = Done (I have *already* eaten). **Yet** = Not done, but waiting to happen (I haven't eaten *yet*).

"Have you finished your homework **yet**?"

!

Bragging Rights

Crazy Experiences
  • I have met a famous person.
  • I have flown in a helicopter.
  • I have eaten exotic food.
Life Milestones
  • I have travelled abroad.
  • I have won a competition.
  • I have learned a language.

Street Tip:

To sound like a fast native speaker, contract the 'have'. "I have been" becomes "**I've been**". "She has seen" becomes "**She's seen**".

The Bragging Mission

Record a voice note about an amazing experience! Say: "I have [V3 Verb] to [Place]. I went there in [Year], and it was [Adjective]!"
