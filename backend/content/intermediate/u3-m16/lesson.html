"For generations, the 'happily-ever-after' narrative has strictly involved two people. However, a growing number of couples are choosing to open up their relationships to explore ethical non-monogamy.

These arrangements require intense communication, brutal honesty, and strict rule-setting. Furthermore, partners must constantly navigate their own deep-seated insecurities without getting defensive.

Critics argue that open relationships are just an excuse to hook up with other people without feeling guilty—a way to have your cake and eat it too. But practitioners claim it actually eliminates the desire to cheat on your partner, creating a much more honest and resilient bond."

HOST: "Welcome back to the podcast. Today, we're discussing the taboo of polyamory. When a couple decides to open up their marriage, society instantly assumes they are doomed to fail.

But my guest today argues the exact opposite. He claims that traditional monogamy breeds secrecy—and furthermore, it places an impossible burden on one single person to fulfill all of your emotional needs."

GUEST: "Exactly. When you remove the expectation of exclusivity, you remove the urge to cheat on your partner. It requires massive emotional maturity to handle the jealousy.

You can't just treat people like toys and have your cake and eat it too. It's about honesty."
