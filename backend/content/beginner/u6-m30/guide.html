Ownership Nuggets

🏷️

The Bodyguards (Adjectives)

Words like **My, Your, His, Her**. Think of them as bodyguards. They are hungry and they ALWAYS need a noun right next to them to protect.

"This is **my** phone." (Not: This is my ❌)

The Solo Stars (Pronouns)

Words like **Mine, Yours, Hers, Ours**. They already have the noun "swallowed" inside of them, so they stand completely ALONE.

"That phone is **mine**." / "Is it **yours**?"

The "S" Rule

Notice how almost all pronouns are just the adjective with an **'S'** added to the end? (Your ➔ Yours, Her ➔ Hers, Our ➔ Ours). The only weird one is My ➔ Mine!

"Their house is big, but **theirs** is bigger."

The "His" Exception

For a guy, the word doesn't change! It's **his** for both the Adjective and the Pronoun. Never say "hises"!

"It is **his** car." ➔ "The car is **his**."

!

The Evolution

Adjectives (+ Noun)
  • This is My bag.
  • Is this Your bag?
  • It is Her bag.
  • That is Our bag.
Pronouns (Stand Alone)
  • The bag is Mine.
  • Is the bag Yours?
  • The bag is Hers.
  • That bag is Ours.

Paisa Secret:

In Spanish, we say "Es mío" (Pronoun) or "Mi carro" (Adjective). In English, the logic is exactly the same! Just memorize the two columns so you don't mix them.

Application Nuggets

⚔️

The "End of Sentence" Test

If the ownership word is the very last word in the sentence (with a period after it), 99% of the time it MUST be a Pronoun!

"I think this jacket is **yours**."

Whose...? (The Interrogation)

To ask who owns something, use **Whose** + Noun. When you answer, you can use either an Adjective or a Pronoun!

"**Whose** keys are these?" ➔ "They are **my** keys." OR "They are **mine**."

The "My One" Trap

A classic Spanglish mistake is saying "The red car is my one" (El mío). Don't say "my one"! Just use the Pronoun directly.

"The red car is **mine**." (Not: is my one ❌)

Its vs. It's

**Its** (no apostrophe) is the Adjective for an animal or thing. **It's** means "It is". There is NO Pronoun version of "It"!

"The dog wagged **its** tail." (Adjective)

!

Side by Side

Option A: Adjective Style
  • "This is my coffee."
  • "Are these your keys?"
  • "That is her jacket."
Option B: Pronoun Style
  • "This coffee is mine."
  • "Are these keys yours?"
  • "That jacket is hers."

Street Tip:

To sound fluent, use the Pronoun to avoid repeating the noun over and over. "I brought my computer. Did you bring **yours**?" (Instead of saying 'your computer' again).

The Claiming Mission

Point to three things in your room. Record a voice note! Say: "This is **my** [Item]. It is **mine**! That is **his/her** [Item]. It is **his/hers**!"
