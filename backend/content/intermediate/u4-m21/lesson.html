THE INVISIBLE LINE

READING & CHECK

"National security is often the primary argument for strict immigration policies; conversely, human rights advocates argue that artificial borders violate our basic right to survive. When people flee from war or famine, they don't care about paperwork or political rhetoric. They are simply trying not to die.

Governments often treat migrants as a burden or a threat. Conversely, economic data frequently shows that immigrants revitalize aging populations and take the difficult jobs that native citizens refuse to do.

If a nation built its wealth by colonizing others, does it now have the right to lock its doors? The hypocrisy of the West is staggering; they demand globalized free trade for their corporations, but they heavily militarize their borders against human beings."

VOCABULARY UPGRADE

Phrasal Verb

FLEE FROM

To run away quickly from danger or disaster.

"Thousands had to flee from the warzone."

Phrasal Verb

KICK OUT

To forcefully remove someone from a place/country.

"The government threatened to kick them out."

Phrasal Verb

SETTLE IN

To become comfortable in a new home or country.

"It took years for the refugees to settle in."

Phrasal Verb

TURN AWAY

To refuse someone entry or deny them help.

"The border guards were told to turn them away."

Idiom

START FROM SCRATCH

To begin completely over with absolutely nothing.

"They lost everything and had to start from scratch."

Slang/Idiom

OFF THE GRID

Living undetected, avoiding government tracking.

"Without a visa, he's forced to live off the grid."

THE AMNESTY DEBATE

ELENA: I can't believe they are planning to kick out families who have lived here for a decade. They are hardworking taxpayers; conversely, billionaires dodge taxes legally and are celebrated.

MARCUS: It's about the rule of law, Elena. You can't just cross a border illegally and expect amnesty. If we don't turn away illegal crossings, the entire system collapses.

ELENA: The "rules" are rigged. People don't uproot their entire lives to live off the grid unless they are desperate. We exploit their cheap labor; conversely, we deny them basic human rights when it's politically convenient.

MARCUS: Look, I sympathize with their struggle to start from scratch, but a nation must have borders. We simply can't save everyone.

DEEP DISCUSSION

If a person enters a country illegally but commits no crimes and pays taxes for 20 years, should they be granted citizenship?

Is it hypocritical for countries that historically colonized others to now strictly militarize and enforce their own borders?

With climate change displacing millions in the coming decades, will borders become completely obsolete or more violently defended?

How would you react if your government ordered you to report an undocumented neighbor or coworker to the authorities?
