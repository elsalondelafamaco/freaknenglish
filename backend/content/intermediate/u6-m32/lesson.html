THE ADDICTION SPECTRUM

THE ARTICLE

For decades, society has viewed addiction as a moral failing; nevertheless, modern psychology classifies it as a complex brain disease. When someone struggles to give up a destructive habit, we often instinctively blame their lack of willpower. Nevertheless, environmental and genetic factors play a massive role in whether someone becomes a "functional" user or a total junkie.

Rather than punishing those who fall back into old patterns, the medical community argues for radical empathy. If a patient is actively trying to cut out toxic substances, throwing them in a jail cell is the worst possible solution. Addiction reshapes the brain's reward centers; therefore, overcoming it is rarely a simple choice.

Even when a person tries to recover, the heavy societal stigma almost guarantees they will fall off the wagon. We must stop treating the fiend as a criminal and start treating them as a patient.

The author argues for 'radical empathy,' claiming that addiction is entirely a brain disease. It is a compelling argument; nevertheless, it completely removes personal responsibility from the equation."

"If a fiend commits a crime to fund their habit, can we simply say, 'It is not their fault, they are sick'? The article ignores the victims. When a junkie continuously lies and steals from their own family, empathy alone does not repair the trauma."

"We must treat the illness, but we cannot completely cut out the concept of personal accountability."
