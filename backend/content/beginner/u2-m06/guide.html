Routine Nuggets

⏰

The Simple Present

Use this tense to talk about daily routines, habits, and facts. It is NOT for things happening exactly right now.

"I **wake up** at 7 AM." / "They **play** soccer."

The "S" Thief (He / She / It)

When the doer of the action is He, She, or It, the verb MUST steal an 'S' at the end. This is the golden rule of English!

"Gelmy **works** hard." / "Titó **sleeps** all day."

The "ES" Exception

If the verb ends in **o, ch, sh, ss, x, or z**, just adding 'S' looks weird. So, we add **ES**.

"He **goes**." / "She **watches** TV." / "It **fixes**."

The "Y" Flip

If the verb ends in a consonant + Y (like *study* or *cry*), drop the 'Y' and add **IES**.

"He **studies** English." / "The baby **cries**."

!

The Roster

The Lazy Group (No changes)
  • I work.
  • You work.
  • We / They work.
The VIP Group (+ S)
  • He works.
  • She works.
  • It works.

Paisa Secret:

In Spanish, the verb changes for EVERY person (yo corro, tú corres, él corre). In English, it only changes for He/She/It. The rest of them stay exactly the same!

Helper Nuggets

🛑

The Negative Wall (Don't / Doesn't)

Never say "He no work". You must use a helper! Use **Don't** for I/You/We/They. Use **Doesn't** for He/She/It.

"I **don't** care." / "She **doesn't** care."

The Return of the 'S'

CRUCIAL! When you use *Doesn't* or *Does*, the main action verb goes back to normal. The helper already stole the 'S'!

"He doesn't **work**." (Not: He doesn't works ❌)

The Interrogation (Do / Does)

To ask a Yes/No question, put **Do** or **Does** at the very front of the sentence.

"**Do** you study?" / "**Does** he study?"

Short Answers

Don't repeat the whole sentence. Just reply with the helper verb they asked you with.

"Do you sleep?" ➔ "**Yes, I do.** / **No, I don't.**"

!

The Matrix

Negatives (-)
  • I / You / We / They + Don't + Verb
  • He / She / It + Doesn't + Verb (No S)
Questions (?)
  • Do + You / They + Verb ?
  • Does + He / She / It + Verb ? (No S)

Street Tip:

In English, "Do" and "Does" are like ghosts. In positive sentences, they are invisible. But in negatives and questions, they appear to help the verb!

The Gossip Mission

Record a voice note about your daily routine versus a friend's. Say: "I [Action], but I don't [Action]. My friend [Action+S], but he/she doesn't [Action]!"
