SYSTEMIC ROT

READING & CHECK

"We love to think of corruption as something that only happens in dark alleys or villainous lairs; however, the reality is much more mundane. It happens in boardrooms, government offices, and through our screens. Modern scams are highly sophisticated: they rely on our trust, fear, and desire for quick success.

When a politician accepts a 'donation' from a massive corporation, we politely call it lobbying. Thus, the system normalizes bribery by simply rebranding it. When a tech company sells our private data without explicit consent, they hide behind a fifty-page terms of service agreement.

Systemic rot isn't always illegal. Often, the biggest scams are entirely within the boundaries of the law. Hence, the true danger lies not in the broken rules, but in the rules themselves."

VOCABULARY UPGRADE

Phrasal Verb

COVER UP

To hide a mistake or crime.

"The mayor tried to cover up the scandal."

Phrasal Verb

RIP OFF

To cheat someone by overcharging them.

"That tourist trap completely ripped us off."

Phrasal Verb

PAY OFF

To bribe someone to keep quiet or do a favor.

"They had to pay off the inspector."

Idiom

UNDER THE TABLE

Secretly and illegally (often referring to money).

"He earns a lot, but it's all under the table."

Slang

SHADY

Suspicious, dishonest, or untrustworthy.

"I wouldn't invest; the deal seems shady."

Slang

SCAMMY

Resembling a fraudulent scheme or trick.

"That email asking for money looks scammy."

THE CORRUPTION DEBATE

LISA: Did you see the news about the governor? It turns out the massive construction contract was awarded to his brother-in-law's firm. It’s entirely under the table.

DAVID: I saw it. But honestly, are you surprised? That’s just how politics works. You pay off the right people, and you get the contract. It's business.

LISA: So we’re just supposed to accept it? It’s completely shady. They are using taxpayer money to enrich themselves; hence, the public infrastructure continues to crumble while they get rich.

DAVID: I'm not saying it's morally right, but everyone does it. If he hadn't given it to his brother-in-law, another politician would have given it to their own friend. Thus, the system perpetuates itself. It's a game: you either play it or you lose.

DEEP DISCUSSION

If a corrupt politician actually improves the economy and lowers crime, should we care that they take money under the table?

Is it worse for a desperate citizen to steal groceries, or for a billionaire to evade millions in taxes through legal loopholes?

Have you ever encountered a situation where paying a small bribe was the *only* way to get something essential done?

As artificial intelligence advances, do you think scams will become impossible to detect, or will we develop better tools to fight them?
