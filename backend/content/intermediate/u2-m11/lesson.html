THE ILLUSION

CASE STUDY
@MiddleClassMillennial Confession

"I did everything I was supposed to do. I went to college, got a degree, and landed a 'good' corporate job. Undoubtedly, I am working twice as hard as my parents did at my age. Indeed, my productivity is higher than ever before.

But despite all this effort, I can barely afford rent, let alone buy a house. Meanwhile, the executives at my company—who inherited their wealth—just bought their third yachts. We are told that if we just work hard, we will succeed. That is a complete lie.

The game is rigged to keep the middle class exhausted and poor. Am I crazy, or is the system designed to make us fail?"

DETAIL
  • / What exact steps did the writer take to supposedly ensure success?
  • / How did the writer use the Em Dash (—) to add extra, dramatic information?
THINKING
  • / Do you agree that the system is intentionally designed to keep people exhausted?
  • / Why do we continue to believe the myth that "hard work equals extreme wealth"?

THE ACTIVIST

TRUE OR FALSE?

Listen to the activist's speech. Decide if the statements are True (T) or False (F).

  • The speaker believes that billionaires earn their money by working billions of times harder than regular people.
  • According to the activist, the idea that wealth comes strictly from hard work is a dangerous myth.
  • The speaker claims that the ultra-rich build empires primarily by paying their fair share of taxes.
  • The activist argues that a CEO making 400 times more than an employee is an example of theft.
REACTION
  • / Does an activist's angry tone make their message more or less effective?
  • / Do you agree that massive corporate profit is a form of "theft" from the workers?
SPEECH TRANSCRIPT

"Listen to me! We are constantly told that wealth is the direct result of hard work. That is a myth—a dangerous illusion designed to keep you quiet. Undoubtedly, the people working the hardest in our society are the ones struggling the most to survive.

Look at the billionaires! They didn't earn their money by working billions of times harder than a nurse or a teacher. Indeed, they built their empires by exploiting cheap labor—and avoiding taxes.

When a CEO makes 400 times more than their average worker, that isn't a successful economy. That is theft. We need to stop worshipping the ultra-rich and start demanding an economy that actually works for everyone!"
