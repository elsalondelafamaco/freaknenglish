THE FAILED EXPERIMENT

READING & CHECK

For decades, governments have attempted to eradicate narcotics through violent prohibition (the so-called "War on Drugs"). This approach has spectacularly failed; specifically, it has empowered ruthless cartels and criminalized public health issues.

When authorities aggressively crack down on illegal substances (namely, marijuana and psychedelics), they often completely ignore the much deadlier crisis of legally prescribed pharmaceuticals that ruin millions of lives.

Some experts argue we should phase out prohibition entirely. If an adult wants to alter their own consciousness on a weekend, is it truly the government's job to stop them, or is bodily autonomy the ultimate human right?

VOCABULARY UPGRADE

Phrasal Verb

CRACK DOWN

To take severe or stricter measures against something.

"The police decided to crack down on illegal racing."

Phrasal Verb

HOOKED ON

To be deeply addicted to a substance or behavior.

"He got hooked on painkillers after the surgery."

Phrasal Verb

PHASE OUT

To gradually stop using or providing something.

"They will phase out the old laws over five years."

Idiom

GO COLD TURKEY

To stop an addiction suddenly and completely.

"After years of smoking, she just went cold turkey."

Slang

STONER

A person who regularly smokes marijuana.

"He sleeps till noon and plays Xbox; he's a total stoner."

Slang

"I couldn't even follow the movie, I was completely baked."

THE LEGALIZATION DEBATE

NATE: The hypocrisy is insane. The state sells us alcohol—which ruins millions of lives—but wants to crack down on nature. People should be allowed to choose, namely because prohibition clearly doesn't work.

SARAH: But total legalization is dangerous. It's not just about a harmless stoner getting baked on a Friday night (though that's the cliché). We are talking about highly addictive chemicals. You can't expect a society of people hooked on narcotics to just function normally.

NATE: But making it illegal doesn't stop them! If we phase out the criminal element, we can treat addiction as a health issue (rather than a crime). You can't force people to go cold turkey in a prison cell and call it rehabilitation.
